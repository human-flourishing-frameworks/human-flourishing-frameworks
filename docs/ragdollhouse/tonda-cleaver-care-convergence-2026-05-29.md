## 1) Highest-yield clarifiers (ask hospital team)

These reduce uncertainty fast and determine what “sick” likely means in a post-op setting:

- Exact pancreas surgery type: distal pancreatectomy vs other; operative note summary.
- Indication for surgery: pancreatitis, mass/cyst, malignancy workup, infection, other.
- Current working diagnosis for this admission: infection/sepsis, leak/fistula, bleeding, ileus/obstruction, poor pain/nausea control, malnutrition, uncontrolled glucose, other.
- Current imaging/labs highlights: CT findings, WBC trend, cultures, drain output (if present), bilirubin/LFTs, glucose.
- Current devices: abdominal drain(s), central line, feeding tube/TPN.
- Discharge plan: follow-up services, diet instructions, warning signs, who to call.

## 2) Post-op “why still so sick?” check (non-diagnostic)

After pancreas + gallbladder surgery, the most common high-impact buckets to explicitly confirm/rule out:

### A) Leak/fistula / intra-abdominal infection
Ask directly if they have evaluated for pancreatic leak/fistula and intra-abdominal collections/abscess.

### B) Nutrition + digestion failure
If there is ongoing weight loss, diarrhea/greasy stools, cramping/bloating, or inability to tolerate food:
- ask if **pancreatic enzyme replacement** is indicated,
- ask for a specific diet plan and calorie/protein targets.

### C) Glucose instability
Removal of pancreatic tissue can precipitate or worsen diabetes. Ask what inpatient glucose has been, and what outpatient monitoring plan is.

### D) Gallbladder removal adjustment/complications
Most people need time for bile flow adjustment; ask what diet guidance applies and what complications have been ruled out (bile leak, duct issues, pancreatitis).

## 3) Red flags (urgent escalation)

If any are present, treat as urgent and escalate to the hospital team immediately:
- fever/chills, confusion, shortness of breath, fainting
- worsening belly pain/distension, persistent vomiting
- jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), black/tarry stools, uncontrolled bleeding

## 4) One-page “ask list” for family advocates

Use this verbatim with the inpatient team:

1. “What is the current diagnosis causing her to be sick right now?”
2. “Has a pancreatic leak/fistula been ruled out? What do the CT and drain/labs show?”
3. “What is the nutrition plan (diet/TPN/feeding tube), and what goals are we targeting?”
4. “Is she having glucose problems? What is the plan after discharge?”
5. “What specific symptoms should trigger ER return vs call-the-surgeon?”
6. “Who is the single point of contact (surgeon team vs hospitalist vs GI)?”
